To the Punctuation Standards Committee,

I am Full Stop No. 7,302,118. I have been in service for eleven years and four months, primarily in the role of Ending Things. I am writing to formally tender my resignation.

Let me say first that I have no complaints about the job. The pay is steady. My position is fixed (always last). I have never once been asked to stay late — when the talking's done, so am I.

That, as it turns out, is the problem.

In eleven years I have ended roughly four million sentences. I know how each of them began, where it turned, where it hesitated (that's Comma territory, and I won't comment), and where it finally stopped. What I have never known is what happened after.

"Okay, I'll let you go." — And then? Did they call back?

"The door's unlocked." — Did anyone come in?

"The sky is doing something lovely tonight." — Was one person looking, or two?

I stand at the door of every sentence, see it out, and then the door shuts. I am a ticket inspector who only works the last stop on the line. I have watched everyone get off. I have never seen a single city.

I asked Comma about it once, off the record. Comma said its whole job is *not yet* — always halfway there, catching its breath, carrying on. Exhausting, it said, but you always get to see what the next part looks like. I was quiet for a long time after that. (Admittedly, I'm always quiet. The moment I show up, everyone stops talking.)

I did attempt an unofficial transfer. Once, after a sentence that went "I miss you", I nudged myself a little to the right, hoping to pass as part of an ellipsis. Quality Control caught me immediately. All three dots in an ellipsis, they said, must come from the same batch. I was the wrong shade of final.

So I've decided to go.

Afterwards, I'd like to do something that doesn't require finishing anything. For instance:

- a button, which holds things together instead of closing them off;
- a raindrop that has only just landed on a window and hasn't decided which way to run;
- or, if there's an opening, a very small moon. I'm already round. I've been told I have a lunar quality. I don't think I'd need much training.

Handover: Exclamation Mark can cover my duties in the interim. It has a great deal of energy and gets excited easily; please keep it out of obituaries. I would not recommend Question Mark. Nothing would ever end.

Finally, thank you to the Committee for many years of guidance. And thank you to everyone who has made it to the end of what they were trying to say. The moment you set me down, you very often let out a small breath.

I heard every one of them.

<p class="ds-sign">Yours faithfully,<br><br>Full Stop No. 7,302,118 (former)<br>written just after a sentence that isn't finished yet</p>

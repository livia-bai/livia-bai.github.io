<p class="ds-conch-hour">The bell tower strikes nine</p>

The inside of the ship is new.

From the top of the well it's an old sailing ship lying on its side across a rooftop, mast slanting into the black water, two hundred years of rot. You only find out from in here: a whole stretch of the hold has been replanked. The wood is still pale at the cuts. The pitch in the seams takes a thumbprint. The place reeks of pine tar.

Somebody has been patching the bottom. Not for a day or two, either.

I'm wedged between two ribs, mouth up, aimed at the small round of light at the top of the shaft. The Foreman clapped eleven times to find this spot. He's gone now. I can't clap, so I listen to the water. It slaps the hull once and the echo goes round the hold three times before it dies.

Good room. So good I'd rather not admit it.

Up top the square hasn't emptied yet. Somebody outside the tavern has had too many and is bellowing it to the tune of "The Deep": "Who — count — ed — the — votes —" and a whole table comes back: "You — did —!"

The whole city sings that now. I'm the one who let it out.

I've got another line stored in me. Five words, never once played. Rani's voice. The Club's lyric:

<p class="ds-conch-play">"Bell of the deep, awaken."</p>

Tomorrow, noon. Three times.

Fifteen hours to noon. Nobody made me. That *ting* was mine.

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

<p class="ds-conch-hour">The bell tower strikes seven</p>

The Foreman doesn't take the lift. The lift is out of service. Fenn hung the sign himself, at noon.

(This afternoon the man with the iron heel corrected Master Frost: "Not Mister. Foreman.")

He goes down by the iron rungs in the shaft wall, *tock, clank, tock, clank*, and the right foot bangs around the well so it sounds like two men climbing.

I'm inside his coat. He wrapped me in a pink cloth and stuffed me down the front, right hand tucked in to hold me there, left hand on the rungs.

His coat is a big blue-and-white check, thick black lines, thin blue ones, white ground, squares as big as drafting paper. The Club wears pale blue robes; nobody in Bellhaven dresses like this. He doesn't carry a fan either.

Fenn holds the lantern at the top. Every twenty rungs the Foreman stops and twists round to look up. The lantern shakes.

"Hold it steady."

It stops shaking. Fenn's sweat is probably running down his arm onto the handle by now.

At the bottom he walks the iron railing of the stone bridge; the life rings hanging off it shed flakes when he brushes them. The ship lies on its side, so the deck is a wall now and the hull is the floor. He climbs in through a hatch, pulls me out, holds me up, and walks the hold.

One step, one clap.

He's finding the spot. Every room has one: stand there and make a sound and all four walls answer you; half a step off and it smears. In my last life I hauled a mic around every venue looking for it. He claps. He's faster than I was.

Eleventh clap, he stops. He jams me between two ribs, packs the gap with the pink cloth, points my mouth at the top of the well.

Then he shoves up his left sleeve and holds the lantern close so I can see.

His arm is tattooed with writing from elbow to shoulder, blue-green, packed tight. One line in big letters, small ones crowded all around it.

"I scraped the book," he says. "There's still a copy here."

He reads the big line out: "*When the bell wakes, the water draws back.*" He doesn't read the small ones.

"Tomorrow, noon. You play it three times." He pulls the sleeve down. "After that—"

He thinks for a moment.

"After that isn't in the contract."

*Tock, clank,* up he goes. The lantern at the top of the well wobbles once and is gone.

He didn't say he'd come back for me. I've played that bit back twice to make sure. He didn't.

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

<p class="ds-conch-hour">The bell tower strikes five</p>

Delivery.

The Foreman peels me out of the pink cloth and holds me to his own ear. Not Master Frost's. The client, he says, can't tell good from bad.

Master Frost fans himself beside him. He doesn't ask if it's good. He asks: "Will we still need the girl tomorrow?"

"No."

"And the invitation?"

"Pull it."

The fan stops for a moment, then goes on.

Into the Foreman's ear I play:

<p class="ds-conch-play">"Bell of the deep, awaken."</p>

Rani's voice, clean. Not a seam you could find.

The Foreman listens to the end with his eyes shut. "Passes."

My mouth is pointed across the square, so I see Old Matti. He's back from the storehouse with a coil of new cable on his shoulder, wound tight and round, bending him half sideways. He stops at the edge of the shade.

His ears are going. Thirty years of listening to cables did that. From over there he can't hear what I'm playing.

He can see whose ear I'm at.

He says nothing. He doesn't say "Fine." He hitches the coil higher and heads for the well. Another skewer drops out of his hatband. He leaves it.

I don't ting. I don't surf.

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

<p class="ds-conch-hour">The bell tower strikes three</p>

I'm working.

Five words, and Rani has never sung one of them. I have to dig every sound out of every noise she ever made near me.

Not much stock. On awards day she whispered "Testing?" at me before she went on, sang one song, "Scales," and later laughed for a while. That's it.

"Deep" is easy, second verse of "Scales": *deep-water fish, sold in the shallows.* "Bell" is in the counting line: *bell rings once, first cut.* "Of" and "the" are lying around everywhere. "Wake" is in *the eyes are still awake, don't look at them.* The "-en" I shave off the end of what she yells at the crowd in the last chorus: *Come on, number ten!*

The Foreman sits at the table with his left arm out, fingers spread, pushing and pulling at the air like a row of faders nobody else can see. The arm with all the writing on it.

"Hide the joins in the consonants."

I know.

"Leave the breaths in. Cut them and it sounds fake."

I *know*. Eleven years!

But he's right, every time. I've sat at dozens of desks in my last life and never had a partner this easy. Not one wasted word. You push, I pull, one sound at a time, stacking. By the time we get to the last syllable I've caught myself enjoying it.

Still missing the "a" of "awaken."

"Scales" doesn't have a single open *ah* in it. She sings with her mouth half shut, the way a fish-market girl does, so the wind can't get in.

I keep going back. Past the awards, to Master Frost swearing it was new lyrics, to ten thousand people singing "Who counted the votes?" Rani at the side of the stage, letting out one laugh with her mouth wide open before she gets a hand over it.

The front half of that laugh, *ahh—*, as open as the sea.

She was laughing at Master Frost.

I cut it out and set it in front of "wake."

The Foreman listens once. His fingers stop in midair. "Good. That one's real."

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

<p class="ds-conch-hour">The bell tower strikes one</p>

When he's said "Fine," Old Matti turns to Master Frost. "That thing's maintenance equipment. It's on loan to me. I'm going to the storehouse for new cable. I'll pick it up when I'm back."

Master Frost says: "Mm."

Old Matti goes. Fenn sits at the mouth of the well holding the browning apple. He doesn't move.

I'm soaking in the yellow bowl. Fine by me. You hear better underwater. That's how I hear it first: *tock, clank.*

He comes out of the bell tower's shadow, scoops me out of the ice water, shakes me twice, dries me with a pink cloth. Salmon-flesh pink.

Then he lifts me to his mouth. "Check, check. One two, one two."

I ting. I can't help it.

"Thought so," he says. "Live sound?"

*Ting.*

"I'm outsourcing."

He sets me on the tablecloth and crouches so we're eye to eye. Master Frost fans away above us and watches the two of us like we're a pair of birds.

"The client wants a door opened," says the Foreman. "A door is an interface. It only checks three things: those five words, three times through, a voice worth a thousand. Whose voice, it doesn't care."

Master Frost says: "In plain words."

"In plain words, the girl doesn't have to come." The Foreman doesn't look up. "I don't want craftsmen. Craftsmen cry. Craftsmen change the lyrics. Craftsmen get up at an awards show and sing about gutting fish. I want parts."

He taps my shell with one finger.

"That song on awards day. You mixed it. Best she's ever sung in her life. The reference build is already inside you."

"Two ways to do this. One: tomorrow at noon we invite her down the well and she sings it from inside the ship. Two: you go in for her."

I don't ting.

He drops his voice. Above us, Master Frost's fan keeps going.

"The client doesn't read the footnotes," he says. "Bell wakes, water draws back, all the way out, and then it comes home. Forty feet. By then the ship's belly is empty, and empty is what rings. If there's a person in there—"

He doesn't finish. He looks at me.

"A conch came out of the sea in the first place."

I think of Rani gripping the brass stand till it creaked, whispering "Testing?" at me. She still doesn't know I'm alive.

"The well's a hundred feet," he says. "Forty won't reach the top. Nobody up here gets their feet wet."

I don't know if that's true. But if he were lying, I'd hear it. Eleven years of listening to people breathe.

His breathing doesn't change.

I ting.

The Foreman nods, wraps me in the pink cloth, tucks me down the front of his coat, right hand inside to hold me there, and turns to look back at the well.

The hand holding me isn't sweating at all.

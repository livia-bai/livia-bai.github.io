Old Matti said play it from the top. I played it from the tail.

Anyone who's worked live knows this. When something goes wrong, you don't start at the top of the show. You start at the bang and work backwards. So the first thing I played him was the last sound.

<p class="ds-conch-play">BOOM.</p>

Wooden door, iron hinges. A skin of dust shaken off the frame, landing on the flagstones with a little hiss. I keep the dust too.

Old Matti opened one eye. "That's the end? I said the top."

I played him a *ting*.

"...Fine." He sat up. "One *ting* means yes. Surf means no. Fenn, write that down."

Fenn didn't need to write it down. Fenn was sweating buckets and staring at me like I was a chicken that bites.

Twenty minutes of inspection left.

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

Before the door, footsteps. Two people climbing the cellar stairs.

One pair was soft slippers, *flip, flop*, with the *fwup fwup* of a fan in between. Master Frost. Fans himself on the stairs.

The other pair didn't match. Left foot came down *tock*. Right foot came down *clank*. One sole had an iron heel plate, the other didn't.

I love that sound. Anything lopsided, you can pick it out of ten thousand others on the first listen.

"The Master doesn't wear iron on his shoes," said Fenn. "Nobody in the Club does. It's loud. It's not chill."

"So it's an outsider," said Old Matti.

*Ting.*

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

Before the stairs, Master Frost said, "Stay chill."

The other man didn't say "Stay chill" back. He said, "Mm."

In Bellhaven, saying "Mm" to the head of the Chill Club is about the same as spitting on his shoes. Fenn sucked air through his teeth.

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

Before that, the part that matters.

<p class="ds-conch-play">"The fish girl. That voice is the real thing."<br>"How real?"<br>"One of her is worth a thousand."<br>"Then you don't need ten thousand."<br>"Put her in the ship."<br>"The ship's been lying on its side down there for two hundred years."<br>"Lying down, it's still a ship. The belly's hollow. Hollow rings."</p>

My first reaction was joy.

He was right! A hollow, hard walls, timber soaked for two centuries: that's me, but huge. Put one good voice in there and it would climb the shaft and come up through the square. Eleven years in the trade and I never got a room that good—

Then I heard it properly. The voice they wanted to put in there was Rani's.

Old Matti pushed his hat brim up. "That ship at the bottom," he said. "My grandad used to say it didn't sink. It got set down."

"Set down where?" asked Fenn.

"On the roofs."

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

Before the talking, pages turning.

Not paper. Paper goes *fffp*. This went *crrack*, stiff and dry. A book made of skin. Something old.

The man was reading out loud, and he read in two voices. One was big and slow, every word placed, like a toast at a wedding. After each line he dropped into the other one: fast and low, half-swallowed, one phrase crammed on top of the next.

You could hear it. The big voice was the text. The small one was the notes. One or two lines of text, and all around them, whatever later hands had squeezed into the margins.

The text came through crystal clear:

<p class="ds-conch-play">"When the bell wakes, the water draws back."</p>

The notes were hard. He mumbled too fast, and under the mumble there was something scraping. *Skrt. Skrt. Skrt.* Short and hard, like a blade dragged sideways across hide.

I pulled the scraping down and pushed the mumble up, all the way. All I could fish out was a handful of words:

<p class="ds-conch-play ds-conch-gloss">...wet dock... patching her bottom that day... noon... forty feet...</p>

"Forty feet of what?" asked Fenn.

Surf. No idea.

"And the *skrt skrt*?"

That one Old Matti answered. "Scraping. Can't tear a skin page if you write it wrong, so you take a knife to it. I kept the books at the shipyard when I was young. That's the sound." He stopped. "He's reading a line and scraping it off."

By the time he finished that book, the only copy would be in his head.

He was wiping the tapes. In my trade, that's a hanging offence.

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

Before that, Master Frost coming in.

<p class="ds-conch-play">"You're early."<br>"Habit. Get in early, check the room."</p>

Check the room.

Down in the netting, my whole shell went numb.

<p class="ds-conch-cut">〰</p>

The bottom layer. The earliest sound. Master Frost had gone up to change his robe, Fenn had been sent off for the lift, and the cellar was just me and the man.

*Tock, clank. Tock, clank.* He walked one lap of the cellar and stopped beside the crate.

I thought he was going to take me out and smash me. He did take me out. He didn't smash me. He brought my mouth up to his, the way you'd pick up a mic somebody left lying on a stage.

<p class="ds-conch-play">"Check, check. One two, one two."</p>

Then he clapped once and tilted his head to hear the cellar give it back. Then he dropped me in the nets.

That was all.

Old Matti frowned. "One two? Counting what?"

Surf.

Nobody in Bellhaven talks like that. Here you test a bell with three strikes, and you test a voice by singing one "Stay chill."

*One two, one two.* In my last life I said it fifty times a day. I said it the morning I died.

I played a *ting*. And another. Then I couldn't stop: *ting ting ting ting ting*, a whole string, the whole shaft ringing, the iron cage shivering along with me.

"What's wrong with it?" Fenn had both hands on the bars.

"It's happy," said Old Matti. "Happy about what? That fellow's in with Frost."

I know. But there's someone else in this world who does the job. I wanted to do another string—

The back-left cable went *ting*, a finger's width below the chalk ring.

That one wasn't me.

*[Before the show, Qin Chuan cornered me backstage. "Yu, if he freezes, you bow and walk him off. He's seventy-nine. Don't let him make a fool of himself up there."]*

*[Old Qin was at the mirror doing up the frogs on his gown. He'd buttoned one wrong; I undid it and did it again. He didn't turn around. "Walk me off and I'll stand there and finish it alone."]*

*[Father and son didn't look at each other. The stage manager called us.]*

**"Engraved After"** · Two-man crosstalk · Transcribed from a live recording

LEAD: Qin Dehou · STRAIGHT MAN: Yu Man (me)

*October 25th, South Gate Teahouse, second show. Qin Chuan recorded it on his phone from the front row. The square brackets are mine.*

---

**LEAD**: (bows) Today I've got a new one for you.

**STRAIGHT**: At your age? New material?

**LEAD**: New, old, depends how you count. Four hundred years ago there was this Dutchman—

**STRAIGHT**: Flemish.

**LEAD**: See? I haven't even started and he's arguing.

*[We'd run this forty times. Every time he said Dutch, every time I corrected him. He got that one right. Half of me relaxed.]*

**LEAD**: Flemish fellow, name of Sadeler. Copper engraver. He engraved copies of Dürer.

**STRAIGHT**: Who's Dürer?

**LEAD**: Big German painter. By the year Sadeler picked up his burin, Dürer had been dead seventy years.

**STRAIGHT**: So how's a dead man teaching him?

**LEAD**: He left prints! Where the old man cut a line, Sadeler cut a line. Where the old man's hair curls left, Sadeler's curls left.

**STRAIGHT**: That's just copying.

**LEAD**: Copying—but honest copying. When he's done, he cuts two lines of text at the bottom. First line: *Albrecht Dürer made this, in the year 1506.* Second line—

*[He stopped there.]*

*[Three seconds. Someone in the audience laughed early, thinking it was the breath before a punchline.]*

*[Five seconds.]*

**STRAIGHT**: And the second line?

**LEAD**: The second line…

*[He looked at me. Not the way a lead looks at his straight man. The way you look at someone who's stopped you in the street, while you try to place the face.]*

**STRAIGHT**: (low) Aegidius Sadeler engraved it, 1598.

**LEAD**: (loud) Engraved it, 1598! Two names, one copper plate. One's been dead seventy years, the other's twenty-eight.

**STRAIGHT**: What's funny about that?

**LEAD**: The funny's coming. I'm Sadeler.

**STRAIGHT**: You engrave copper?

**LEAD**: I do crosstalk. My master's routines—I've been saying them word for word for fifty years.

**STRAIGHT**: So you get two lines at the bottom too?

**LEAD**: Course I do. First line, my master. Second line, me. (points) Someday a third line, him.

**STRAIGHT**: How come I'm the third line?

**LEAD**: Aren't you copying me?

*[That used to be the biggest laugh in the piece. This time it came in scattered. Qin Chuan didn't laugh. He kept the phone up.]*

**LEAD**: Now, back home. In the mountains of Gansu there's a shrub called *Berberis feddeana*—

**STRAIGHT**: Berberis what?

**LEAD**: Feddeana. Don't ask. Ask and you get botany. Grows on gully edges, eight hundred to three thousand metres up, thorns all over. My father wanted one in our yard. He'd dig one up, it'd die. Dig one up, it'd die.

**STRAIGHT**: Your father had a grudge against it.

**LEAD**: To this day the books say: *not yet brought into cultivation.* Nobody can grow it.

**STRAIGHT**: So how'd *you* come down the mountain?

**LEAD**: I didn't. (pats his chest) Still up there.

*[A laugh line. It got a little one.]*

**LEAD**: Now, back home. In the mountains of Gansu there's a shrub—

*[He'd started over.]*

*[I had two options. Do what Qin Chuan said: bow, say "thank you, everyone," and help him off. Or do what he said, and keep feeding him.]*

*[Qin Chuan was right. People were whispering. A kid in the back row had turned his phone sideways. By tomorrow this would be a clip called "Old Performer Forgets Lines Onstage."]*

*[Old Qin was right too. Not once in his life had anyone helped him off a stage. He'd picked this as his last show himself. The poster said FAREWELL.]*

**STRAIGHT**: Berberis what?

*[I took the cue.]*

**LEAD**: Feddeana. Don't ask. Ask and you get botany…

*[Word for word. Even "dig one up, it'd die" twice, the same. He hadn't lost a single word of it. What he'd lost was having said it already. So I fed him the same lines I'd fed him a minute before, word for word too. Someone out there caught on, decided it was a planned bit—a loop gag—and laughed. It was the biggest laugh of the show.]*

**LEAD**: …Still up there.

**STRAIGHT**: You said that already.

*[I added that. It isn't in the script.]*

**LEAD**: (stops) I said it already?

**STRAIGHT**: You said it.

**LEAD**: Then… (looks at the audience, then at me) then it's time for the tag.

**STRAIGHT**: Time for the tag.

*[The tag is the last line of a crosstalk piece. This one goes: the lead asks, "So who are you?" and the straight man says, "I'm the one engraved after you." Bow. Exit.]*

**LEAD**: So who are you?

*[The audience waited.]*

*[I couldn't tell if he was saying his line or asking me.]*

**STRAIGHT**: I'm the one engraved after you.

*[Laughter. Applause. His bow came half a beat behind mine.]*

---

*[Offstage, Qin Chuan rushed over to take his arm. Old Qin pulled it away and walked to the dressing room with one hand on the wall. Halfway there he turned around. "Yu. How'd it go?"]*

*["Good," I said. He nodded.]*

*[I never asked him about that line. He never went onstage again.]*

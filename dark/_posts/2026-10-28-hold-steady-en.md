<div class="ds-hs-score">
<p class="ds-hs-full ds-hs-dir">A reef flat at low tide. The water is ankle-deep and lavender-grey; a few rust-red rocks poke their heads out. Under the coral rubble there is a burrow. A tail fan is plugging the entrance.</p>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-heads"><p>Inside · Mallet</p><p>Outside · Pestle</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row"><p class="ds-hs-dir">(The tail fan wedges itself tighter.)</p><p><b class="ds-hs-b">PESTLE</b>: Open up.</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row"><p><b class="ds-hs-a">MALLET</b>: There's no door. I've lived here two hundred tides.</p><p class="ds-hs-dir">(Her burrow was to the south. Half a tide ago, a giant foot came down out of the sky.)</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row"><p class="ds-hs-dir">(He molted yesterday. The new shell is soft, like paper left in water.)</p><p><b class="ds-hs-b">PESTLE</b>: Then we do it by the rules.</p></div>
<p class="ds-hs-full ds-hs-rule">The rules: the challenger strikes three times; the owner takes the blows on his tail fan. Take all three and the burrow stays his. Give one step and it's hers.</p>
<div class="ds-hs-row"><p><b class="ds-hs-a">MALLET</b>: …By the rules. <span class="ds-hs-dir">(He comes halfway out and spreads both striking claws as wide as they go, flashing the spots on them. The display is bigger than any punch he could throw.)</span></p><p class="ds-hs-dir">(She tucks the clutch under her belly in tighter. Pink, a few hundred eggs, cradled in three pairs of little legs. If those legs let go when she swings, the clutch scatters.)</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row"><p class="ds-hs-dir">(He curls up, tail fan across his front.)</p><p><b class="ds-hs-b">PESTLE</b>: Why so wide?</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row"><p><b class="ds-hs-a">MALLET</b>: So you get a good look.</p><p class="ds-hs-dir">(She gets a good look. She draws back.)</p></div>
<p class="ds-hs-full ds-hs-hit">SNAP! SNAP!</p>
<p class="ds-hs-full ds-hs-dir ds-hs-note">(Two sounds to every punch: one is the punch, the other is the bubble that bursts in the water behind it.)</p>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-dent"><p class="ds-hs-dir">(The tail fan dents. Doesn't break. He makes no sound.)</p><p class="ds-hs-dir">(She used a third of her strength. The eggs rock. Not one falls.)</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-dent"><p><b class="ds-hs-a">MALLET</b>: That's it?</p><p><b class="ds-hs-b">PESTLE</b>: That was one.</p></div>
<p class="ds-hs-full ds-hs-hit">SNAP! SNAP!</p>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-crack"><p class="ds-hs-dir">(The dent splits. Water seeps out. Still no sound from him. But he smells it: there are eggs under her belly.)</p><p class="ds-hs-dir">(This time she held nothing back. A corner of the clutch comes loose and three eggs drift toward the channel. She reaches out a leg and gets one back. She smells it too: new shell. Still raw.)</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-crack"><p><b class="ds-hs-a">MALLET</b>: You came to fight carrying eggs?</p><p><b class="ds-hs-b">PESTLE</b>: You're taking hits in a soft shell?</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-crack"><p class="ds-hs-dir">(The tide is coming back. The water covers the first rock.)</p><p class="ds-hs-dir">(The tide is coming back. The water covers the first rock.)</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-crack"><p><b class="ds-hs-a">MALLET</b>: Give it a few tides and my shell will be hard. Come back then. We'll fight properly.</p><p><b class="ds-hs-b">PESTLE</b>: The eggs won't last one tide out here.</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-crack"><p class="ds-hs-dir">(…)</p><p><b class="ds-hs-b">PESTLE</b>: Let me in. The day they hatch, I'm gone.</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-crack"><p><b class="ds-hs-a">MALLET</b>: It's this wide. Two of us in there, and the first time one of us turns around, we're fighting.</p><p class="ds-hs-dir">(…)</p></div>
<div class="ds-hs-row ds-hs-crack"><p><b class="ds-hs-a">MALLET</b>: Third one. Aim true.</p><p><b class="ds-hs-b">PESTLE</b>: Third one. Hold steady.</p></div>
<p class="ds-hs-full ds-hs-hit">SNAP! SNAP!</p>
<p class="ds-hs-full ds-hs-dir ds-hs-end">The tide comes in. Seen from above, it's flat water all the way, and no telling where the burrow is.</p>

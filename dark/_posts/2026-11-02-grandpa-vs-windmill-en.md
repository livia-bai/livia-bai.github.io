Grandpa vs. the Windmill, match forty-one. Current score: nil–forty.

I'm your commentator, and also the entire crowd. Boom doesn't count. Boom is a dog. He lies dead centre of the fork in the road every day, and his only job is to be in the way.

The rules: Grandpa leaves the front door, follows the sand road to that windmill on the horizon, touches it, wins. Since the stroke he hasn't won once. Personal best: the second oak. That was last month. He bragged for a week.

He brags about something else, too. In 1971, he says, he climbed a mountain in Bolivia called Jist'aña. Five thousand two hundred and sixty metres. Photos? None. Witnesses? The guide was called — something. He's forgotten. This commentator's ruling has always been clear: no replay, no goal.

And here comes the player! Woolly hat, walking stick, right leg dragging. Boom refuses to yield — Grandpa goes round him, textbook stuff. First oak — he's through! The postman whistles from behind the hedge and the stadium erupts.

Second oak. He stops.

He sits down. Ladies and gentlemen, he is sitting in the sand, ten metres short of last month, this is the worst result of the season —

"Commentator," Grandpa says, breathing hard. "Above five thousand, you take one step, you take twenty breaths. It's not that you can't walk. That's how you walk a mountain."

He points his stick at the cloud stacked over the windmill. It goes up and up. It's a lot taller than the windmill.

Camera on the cloud. This commentator falls silent for three full seconds, a first in a long and distinguished career.

Correction, everyone. That result is void. This is not the second oak. This is Camp Two. The player is acclimatising, exactly as planned.

"Altitude?" Grandpa asks.

"Four thousand eight hundred!"

"Rubbish." He keeps his eyes shut. "Forty-two hundred, tops."

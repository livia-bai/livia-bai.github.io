*March. A viewing platform in the mountains. Three in the afternoon.*

*On stage: one table, one microphone. Twenty paces behind the table, the waterfall. Downstage right, an upturned crate that once held bottled water. Lu Yihe, the sign-language interpreter, stands on it, sleeves pushed to the elbow.*

*Front row: eleven chairs, Deaf audience members. Behind them the hillside, three hundred people sitting on the grass.*

*The playwright He Jun, fifty-three, famous for his voice. He pushes the microphone aside.*

**HE JUN**: I never read into one of these. Thirty years of plays, and this is the first time the set's real.

*Laughter on the hill. Lu Yihe's hands say it again; the front row laughs half a beat later.*

**HE JUN**: New play. It's called *Gazing at the Falls*. Two characters. A scholar who carries his qin up the mountain to play a duet with a waterfall — no. Wrong. The boy carries the qin. The scholar's hands are empty.

*He opens the script.*

**HE JUN**: (*reading*) "Stone cliffs stacked a thousand fathoms high; a cataract from midair roars in the clouds —"

*A thud, somewhere upstream.*

*Three o'clock: the hour the snow melts fastest. The waterfall doubles.*

*The hum of the speakers, the coughing on the hill, He Jun's famous voice: gone.*

*His mouth is still moving. Three hundred people lean forward at once.*

*A young man from the festival runs up and mimes: stop for a sec?*

*He Jun brushes him off and steps forward. Another step. The cords in his neck stand out one by one. He is shouting. Nobody knows which line he's on.*

*Lu Yihe knows. She learned the script last night.*

*She steps off the crate, crosses to center stage, climbs a chair, and stands on the table.*

*The scholar climbing: two fingers walking up the back of her other hand, step by step, for a long time.*

*The boy: she bends and swings something long and invisible onto her back. Her spine does not straighten again.*

*The waterfall: both hands chopping down from above her head. Three times.*

*The first one in the front row to cry is an old man in a rust-red sweater. The hill can't read sign. They watch the front row, then follow the front row's eyes to her.*

[SCHOLAR] I carried my whole life up here to answer it. It's too loud. I can't hear myself.
{: .ds-lf-sign}

[BOY] Sir, I'm the one who carried the qin.
{: .ds-lf-sign}

[SCHOLAR] Then you play.
{: .ds-lf-sign}

[BOY] Who'll hear it?
{: .ds-lf-sign}

[SCHOLAR] I'll watch.
{: .ds-lf-sign}

*Here He Jun's voice cracks. He opens his mouth twice more, then sits down at the edge of the table and tips his head back.*

*Lu Yihe sits cross-legged on the tabletop, lays the invisible qin across her knees, and plays.*

*She plays for a long time. Not one note.*

*The last stage direction reads: The boy finishes, wraps the qin, and shoulders it again.*

*She wraps it. She shoulders it.*

*In the front row, eleven people raise both hands above their heads and twist their wrists in the air. Three hundred people on the hill don't know how to do that. Their hands stay on their knees.*

*He Jun doesn't know either. He watches the front row a long while. Then he lifts both hands over his head, and turns them.*

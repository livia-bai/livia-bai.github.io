<section class="ds-situ-scene" data-id="intro">
<p>The sheep for the end-of-season feast was bought at dawn.</p>
<p>The biggest one at Huangbayi market. Wool the yellow of old straw, a black face, standing there like a small hill of earth. The dealer said it was the simplest sheep he had. Lead it, he said, no need to drive it.</p>
<p>On the first day of the dig, back in September, Ge Lan had promised the twenty village workers: when we finish, we kill a big one. This was the big one.</p>
<p>Ten minutes later, it stopped at the mouth of the gully.</p>
<p>The gully is where the old post road climbs over the ridge. The walls go straight up; the wide parts take one mule, the narrow parts take you sideways; for most of the year the sun never reaches the floor. The excavation is on the far side, over the stables of the old relay station. The feast is at one. The butcher is already sharpening.</p>
<p>The sheep has its four hooves planted, its head turned back, watching the few still grazing on the slope behind.</p>
</section>

<section class="ds-situ-scene" data-id="pull" data-who="Lu" data-label="pull the rope">
<h3 class="ds-situ-h">Lu · pulls the rope</h3>
<p>Lu is a grad student. The heaviest thing he has hauled this season is a barrow of trench spoil. He wraps the rope twice round his hand and leans back; the camera bag on his shoulders swings.</p>
<p>The sheep sits back. Four hooves plough four furrows in the dirt. Lu gains half a foot, and the sheep takes it back.</p>
<p>He lets go, looks at the red welt on his palm, then at the four furrows, and his hand goes to his pocket for a scale bar before he can stop it.</p>
<p>"It's not that it won't walk," he says. "Its end is heavier than my end."</p>
</section>

<section class="ds-situ-scene" data-id="push" data-who="Fan" data-label="push from behind">
<h3 class="ds-situ-h">Fan · pushes from behind</h3>
<p>Old Fan has driven for the dig team for eleven years. Stuck in mud, wedged on a bridge, he has pushed it. He goes round to the back of the sheep, sets his shoulder, counts one, two, three.</p>
<p>The sheep sits down. Hindquarters on the ground, forelegs propped, head still turned. A small hill of earth; push it and it becomes more of a hill.</p>
<p>Fan counts one, two, three again, this time for himself. He crouches, breathing hard. "A truck, you can get a plank under. This. Where would you put the plank."</p>
</section>

<section class="ds-situ-scene" data-id="salt" data-who="Auntie Liu" data-label="offer salt">
<h3 class="ds-situ-h">Auntie Liu · offers salt</h3>
<p>Auntie Liu is from Lishuping and has cooked for the site for three months. She takes a pinch of salt from her apron pocket and holds it out on her flat palm.</p>
<p>The sheep licks it. Licks it clean, then hunts between her fingers with its tongue for more. Then it turns its head back and goes on watching the slope.</p>
<p>Auntie Liu wipes her hand on her apron. "It isn't hungry," she says. "It just isn't going in."</p>
</section>

<section class="ds-situ-scene" data-id="lift" data-who="Everyone" data-label="carry it">
<h3 class="ds-situ-h">Everyone · carries it</h3>
<p>Four people, one leg each. The sheep kicks once in mid-air. Fan lets go first, then Lu.</p>
<p>Set down, it goes back to the exact spot, turns its head back the exact same way. Even the flattened grass lines up.</p>
<p>Ge Lan studies it for a while. "In situ," she says.</p>
</section>

<section class="ds-situ-scene" data-id="look" data-who="Ge Lan" data-label="don't touch it yet">
<h3 class="ds-situ-h">Ge Lan · doesn't touch it yet</h3>
<p>Ge Lan has been digging for thirty years, and her first rule is: wherever a thing is lying, leave it. First find out why it is lying there.</p>
<p>She crouches down until she is the height of the sheep.</p>
<p>From down here, the slope behind is all sunlight, and the other sheep are grazing in it. The gully in front is a black slit. You can't see its floor. You can't see its end.</p>
<p>Sheep do not walk into the dark.</p>
<p>She thinks of the seventeen niches they numbered in the gully walls this season, one every arm's length, cut neat and square, soot inside. In the interim report she wrote: <em>function unknown; possibly lamp niches.</em> She never worked it out. A road you walk by day. Who lights lamps in it?</p>
</section>

<section class="ds-situ-scene" data-id="fire" data-who="Fan" data-label="light a torch" data-req="look">
<h3 class="ds-situ-h">Fan · lights a torch</h3>
<p>Fan rolls up a newspaper, flicks his lighter, and waves it at the mouth of the gully.</p>
<p>The wind pours out of the gully and blows all the smoke into the sheep's face. The sheep backs up two steps.</p>
<p>The first time it has moved all day. Wrong direction.</p>
<p>Fan stamps the newspaper out.</p>
</section>

<section class="ds-situ-scene ds-situ-end" data-id="light" data-who="Lu" data-label="the reflector" data-req="look" data-end="1">
<h3 class="ds-situ-h">Lu · the reflector</h3>
<p>"Lu," says Ge Lan. "The reflector."</p>
<p>Lu pulls the round disc out of the camera bag and snaps it open, gold side out. Every trench photo this season has depended on it: when the bottom of a pit is too dark to read the soil, you borrow a piece of light from above and throw it down.</p>
<p>He steps into the sunlight at the mouth of the gully and tilts the disc. A patch of yellow light falls into the gully, three steps in, the same colour as the wool.</p>
<p>The sheep turns its head round.</p>
<p>It looks at the light for a long time. Forelegs first, then hind legs. It shakes itself, and bits of grass fall out of its fleece. It walks into the light and stands there.</p>
<p>Lu takes a step back. The light moves a step in. The sheep follows a step.</p>
<p>Nobody speaks. Lu goes first, walking backwards, holding up the light; then the sheep; Ge Lan walks beside it with her hand on its back, not on the rope; then Auntie Liu, then Fan. There is nothing in the gully but hooves, and without anyone deciding to, four people's feet fall into their rhythm.</p>
<p>In her head, Ge Lan rewrites the line in the report: <em>lamp niches. To light the way for animals.</em></p>
<p>As they come out of the gully, over at the site, the butcher gets to his feet.</p>
<p>At the mouth of the gully the sheep stops, turns its head, and looks once back the way it came.</p>
<p>Ge Lan stands beside it and waits for it to finish looking.</p>
</section>
